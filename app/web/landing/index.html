How it works

Three steps. No account to check.

  1. 1

    Store the file

    Your browser computes the file’s SHA-256 fingerprint before anything is uploaded. The file goes to Walrus. The fingerprint goes into the record.

  2. 2

    Share the link

    Each document gets its own check page and a QR code to print on the certificate or send with it.

  3. 3

    Anyone checks a copy

    They drop the file they received. It is hashed on their device and never uploaded. Same fingerprint, same file.

Who is this for?

For people who issue, hold and check documents

  • Clubs and bootcamps

    Issue completion certificates and keep one public list of what you issued.

  • Event organizers

    Attendance certificates for every participant, each with its own check link.

  • Schools and trainers

    Course results and awards that a stranger can check without emailing you.

  • Graduates and job seekers

    Keep your own certificates in a personal vault and put one link on your CV.

  • Employers and recruiters

    Check a candidate’s certificate in seconds. No account, no upload.

  • Small businesses

    Receipts and records you may need to prove later, each kept with its fingerprint.

Check a copy without uploading it

The file someone sent you stays on your device. Credence computes its fingerprint in the browser and compares it with the record. Even a one-byte change shows up, and the result says what that does and does not mean.

Revoke without hiding anything

A revoked document keeps its check page. The page says who revoked it and when, at the top, so anyone holding a copy sees it. It leaves the public list, and the record stays.

Every detail in plain view

No hidden scores, no badges. The blob ID, the fingerprint, the upload time and the end of paid storage are on the page, in a monospace face you can read aloud.

Trust

Credence keeps the records. The files live on Walrus, and nothing on the page asks anyone to take Credence’s word for it.

Stored on Walrus

Files are stored on Walrus, a decentralized storage network, for a paid storage period. Anyone can fetch them by blob ID and hash them again.

Owned by your address

Owners sign in with a Sui wallet, and that wallet pays for storage. Vaults and files belong to that address, so they come back on any device. People checking a document never need a wallet.

FAQ

What does a match prove?

That the file you checked is identical, byte for byte, to the file the vault owner stored on the date shown. It does not prove who issued it. If you know the issuer runs the vault, for example because they link to it from their own website, a match also tells you the file came from them.

Is the file I check uploaded anywhere?

No. “Check a copy” computes the fingerprint in your browser and compares it with the record. The file never leaves your device.

Can anyone create a vault in someone else’s name?

Anyone can type any name, which is why Credence never calls a vault “verified”. Trust a vault when the issuer gave you its link or lists it on a site you know is theirs.

What happens when a document is revoked?

Its check page says “Revoked by the vault owner on” the date, with the reason if one was given, and it leaves the public list. The record stays visible, so a copy can still be checked against it.

Where are files stored, and for how long?

On Walrus, as deletable blobs owned by the owner’s address, for the storage period they choose. Their wallet pays a small fee in WAL; Credence never holds anyone’s funds. Each check page shows the epoch when paid storage ends. Revoking does not delete the file.

Do people who check a document need an account or a wallet?

No. Only vault owners sign in. Anyone with a check link can open it and run both checks.

Which files can I store?

PDF, PNG, JPEG and WebP files, up to 10 MB each. Images preview on the check page; PDFs open in a new tab.

Is Credence open source?

Yes, under the MIT licence. It is built with Flutter and Dartus, the Dart SDK for Walrus.
